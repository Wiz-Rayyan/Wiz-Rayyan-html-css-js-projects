Qul huwa llāhu aḥad
"Say: He is God, the One (Aḥad)"
Keys: Linguistic Theological Rational Scientific
Linguistic analysis
Root: ء–ح–د (a-ḥ-d)
Rāzī distinguishes Aḥad (absolute, indivisible oneness) from Wāḥid (numerical unity). Only God is Aḥad — unparalleled and incomposite.
Theological discourse & argument tree
What does Aḥad exclude? — Rāzī's dialectic
Claim: "Aḥad" denotes absolute oneness
∴ Excludes all multiplicity (ta'addud)
Obj: Does it exclude numerical oneness?
Res: Aḥad transcends number entirely
Claim: "Aḥad" denotes absolute oneness

Comparative tafsīr
Rāzī (d. 1210): Integrates philosophical theology — "Aḥad" excludes composition in essence, attributes, and actions. God's oneness is ontologically necessary, not merely numerical.
Scientific note
Rāzī cites celestial unity — the single outermost sphere (al-falak al-aʿlā) as a cosmological analogue of oneness.
Seven heavens, Throne & Footstool
Cosmological commentary

Rāzī uses the verse on divine oneness to address the cosmological argument: the unified structure of the heavens — concentric spheres with a single primum mobile — points to a single Unmoved Mover.

The ʿArsh (Throne) is the outermost encompassing reality; the Kursī (Footstool) contains all seven heavens. Each heaven bears its own angels and glorification.

Earth sits at the center — not as seat of honor but as the heaviest element, settled by natural motion. The hierarchy itself testifies to a single ordering intelligence.

ʿArsh — divine throne, beyond enumeration
Kursī — encompasses all creation
Seven heavens — spherical, nested
Earth — center by elemental gravity
Theological debate simulator — Is the Qurʾān created?
Position A — Mutakallimūn (Muʿtazila)
The Qurʾān is God's speech, and speech is a temporal act. If God's speech were eternal and uncreated, it would imply a second eternal entity alongside God — violating taʾwīd. The Qurʾān as existing in human utterance and ink is created.
Position B — Ashāʿira / traditional view
God's speech (kalām nafsī) — the eternal meaning — is uncreated and subsists in the divine essence. The letters and sounds (kalām lafẓī) are created vehicles. Denying the eternity of divine speech diminishes the divine attribute of Speech.
Rāzī's synthesis
Rāzī distinguishes between kalām nafsī (the eternal self-subsisting speech of God) and kalām lafẓī (its temporal, created expression in words and sounds). He argues the Ashʿarī position safeguards divine transcendence while accounting for the createdness of the physical Qurʾān. The verse "Qul huwa llāhu Aḥad" itself demonstrates that divine speech, as meaning, is one and indivisible.
