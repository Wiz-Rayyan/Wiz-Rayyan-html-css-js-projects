The surprising science of how we read

By Dr. Amara Nwosu · 5 min read

Reading is not a natural skill. Unlike speech, which humans acquire effortlessly, reading must be taught. Our brains repurpose regions evolved for object recognition to decode written symbols — a remarkable feat of neural flexibility.

Around 15–20% of people have dyslexia. With the right formatting, reading becomes significantly easier and faster for them.

Researchers have found that letter spacing, line length, and background colour all measurably affect reading speed and comprehension. Learn more at reading.org.
