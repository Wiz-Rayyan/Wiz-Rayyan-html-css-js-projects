The original

What unstyled HTML actually looks like

Your browser ships with a default stylesheet. It has always had one. Every heading, every link, every bullet point you've ever seen on an unstyled page was a designer at Netscape or Mozilla making a call in the early 1990s.

You are living inside those calls right now, in every website you've ever visited.

The paradox

Design by omission is still design

Choosing not to style something is not the same as having no aesthetic. It is a deliberate act of refusal. The page communicates something: that content matters more than presentation, that the reader's browser is trusted, that nothing is being sold.

Motherfucking Website understood this before most design manifestos did.

Typography

Times New Roman didn't ask to be here

The default serif was a newspaper font, designed in 1931 to pack more text per column inch in The Times of London. It arrived in browsers by accident of history, shipped with every OS, available everywhere, chosen by default.

Color

Blue links, purple visited, white void

These three colors were chosen from usability research: distinguishable, readable, conventional. They are now so standard they've become invisible — the water fish swim in. Every CSS reset begins by erasing them.
