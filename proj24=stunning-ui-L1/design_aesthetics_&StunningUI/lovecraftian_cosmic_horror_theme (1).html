■ Theology

The Indifferent Gods: Why Mercy Is a Concept Unknown to the Outer Dark

We have studied fourteen forbidden cosmologies. In none of them does a word exist that translates to salvation. In several, the closest equivalent means simply: not yet consumed.

The most merciful thing in the world, I think, is the inability of the human mind to correlate all its contents. We live on a placid island of ignorance in the midst of black seas of infinity, and it was not meant that we should voyage far.

— H.P. Lovecraft, The Call of Cthulhu

The properties of matter as we understand them hold only within the narrow band of reality that human senses can perceive. Beyond that membrane — thinner than thought — lie dimensions in which the laws of physics are suggestions, in which time is texture, in which consciousness is merely one possible configuration of something far older and stranger.

Those who have crossed that membrane and returned — and they are very few — report not terror but a peculiar and devastating sense of scale: the understanding, at last, of how small the word universe truly is.
