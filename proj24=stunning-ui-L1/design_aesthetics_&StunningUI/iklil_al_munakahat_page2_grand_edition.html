Minimum Mahr Threshold · حد المهر الأدنى
HanafiFatawa-i-Alamgiri, Vol. 1 · Ch. 4
The minimum mahr in the Hanafi school is ten dirhams of standard silver. A contract specifying less remains valid, but the wife may demand the minimum ten dirhams. No maximum is stipulated — sky is the limit of generosity.
Ibn Abidin: applies to full-weight Ottoman dirham, not debased later coinage
Revocable Talaq and Iddah Rules · الطلاق الرجعي والعدة
Hanafial-Hidaya, Marghinani
A first or second talaq is revocable within the iddah of three menstrual cycles. Upon expiry without revocation, separation becomes minor irrevocable (ba'in sughra). A third talaq is major irrevocable (ba'in kubra) — remarriage requires a valid intervening marriage and consummation.
Court practice: revocation must be witnessed and recorded in the sijill register
Child Custody After Divorce · حضانة الأطفال
HanafiShafi'iMultaqa al-Abhur
Mother retains custody of sons until age seven, daughters until puberty. Father maintains guardianship (wilaya) and financial maintenance throughout. If mother remarries a non-mahram, custody transfers to paternal grandmother or father. Shafi'i: at age seven or puberty, child may choose parent.
Ottoman court records show extensive litigation over custody disputes in 18th-century Istanbul
Conditions for Plural Marriage · شروط تعدد الزوجات
HanafiMalikiOttoman Qanun, 18th c.
Quranic permission for up to four wives is conditional on equal justice and treatment. Ottoman courts required proof of financial capacity. A first wife could insert a stipulation (shart) in her nikah nameh prohibiting a second marriage — this is enforceable and gives her the right to divorce if violated.
First-wife stipulation clauses appear frequently in 18th-c. Istanbul court registers
Husband's Maintenance Obligation · نفقة الزوجة
HanafiFatawa Qadikhan
Nafaqa (maintenance) includes food, clothing, housing, and domestic help proportional to the husband's means. It begins at contract, not consummation. Failure to provide maintenance entitles the wife to petition for judicial divorce (faskh). Arrears become a debt enforceable against heirs.
Maintenance rates were set by regional market prices — judges consulted price registers (narh defterleri)
Wife-Initiated Divorce (Khul') · الخلع
HanafiShafi'ial-Mabsut, Sarakhsi
A wife may initiate dissolution by returning the mahr to her husband. Khul' requires the husband's acceptance in Hanafi law, making it a negotiated release rather than a unilateral right. The resulting separation is a minor irrevocable ba'in. No iddah maintenance is owed after khul'.
Hanbali and some Maliki positions allow judicial khul' without husband's consent
