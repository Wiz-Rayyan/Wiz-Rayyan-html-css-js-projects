Materia Medica
Rosa damascena
وَرْد · Ward
Petals strengthen the heart and cool fevers. Roots astringent. Seeds of mild purgative virtue. Store dried petals in a sealed vessel away from moisture.
Crocus sativus
زَعْفَران · Za'farān
Stigmas warm and dry in the second degree. Fortify the heart, clarify the complexion. Excessive use stupefies. Beware adulteration with safflower (qurtum).
Aloe succotrina
صَبِر · Ṣabr
Juice cold and dry; purges yellow bile. Applied to eye as collyrium. Leaves heal wounds when applied externally. Do not use in excess as it weakens the stomach.
Mentha piperita
نَعْنَاع · Na'nā'
Warm and dry in third degree. Dissolves flatulence, strengthens the stomach. Fresh mint preferred; wild mint (fūdhanj) stronger — use in lesser quantity.
Cassia senna
سَنَا · Sanā
Leaves purge black and yellow bile. Mecca variety (sanā Makkī) superior. Avoid discoloured leaves; their power is spent. Do not decoct — infuse in cold water to preserve virtue.

I
Ma'jūn al-Qalb
معجون القلب · Cardiac electuary · compound restorative of the vital spirit
Ingredients · Adjzā'
IngredientQuantityPreparationSubst.
Rose petals, driedIf dried roses be unavailable, substitute fresh petals in double quantity. Avoid black-petalled varieties — they lack the warming virtue and may increase melancholy. 4 dramsPowdered fineYes
Saffron stigmasGenuine saffron stains the finger yellow-orange with a bitter taste. Beware of adulteration with safflower (qurtum), which yields no cardiac benefit. ½ dramGround to pasteNo
Gold leaf · waraq al-dhahab 2 grainsDissolved in rosewaterNo
Pearl powder · lū'lū'Pearl from the Persian Gulf is considered finest. Substitute with calcined white coral (marjān) in equal quantity; the virtue is comparable. 1 dramGround with amberYes
Mastic · maṣṭakāPowder in winter months while firm. Store away from heat — mastic softens and loses its powdered form in summer. Chios variety is preferred. 2 dramsPowderedYes
Honey, raw and clarified 3 ouncesLightly warmedYes
Preparation · Tadbīr
Pound the rose petals and mastic separately to fine powders. Dissolve the saffron in two spoonfuls of rosewater over the gentlest heat until it yields a paste of deep gold. Add the pearl powder and gold leaf, stirring with a silver rod. Combine all powders, then incorporate the clarified honey in a slow stream, working the mixture until it achieves the proper electuary consistency — thick, cohesive, and glossy. Seal in a glass vessel (qārūra) and store in a cool location.
Dosage · Jur'ah
1 dram upon waking, dissolved in warm rosewater or pomegranate juice
Therapeutic indications · Manāfi'
Palpitation of the heart Febrile conditions Melancholy Strengthens vital spirit
II
Kuḥl al-'Ayn
كحل العين · Ophthalmic collyrium · preparation for diseases of the eye
Ingredients · Adjzā'
IngredientQuantityPreparationSubst.
Antimony · ithmidThe finest ithmid is the Isfahani variety, dark and without veins. Do not substitute with lead sulfide — it injures the eye and causes blindness over time. 3 dramsGround on porphyry stone until impalpableNo
Aloe succotrina · ṣabr ½ dramDissolved in rosewaterYes
Copper sulfate · zājCypriot variety (qubrusī) is superior to Egyptian. Use in minimal quantity — excess causes severe irritation. Calcine before use to reduce its sharpness. 5 grainsCalcined, then powderedNo
Camphor · kāfūr 2 grainsPowdered separately in cool airYes
Rosewater · mā' al-ward 1 ounceFor trituratingYes
Preparation · Tadbīr
Grind the antimony upon a smooth stone, testing the powder upon the back of the hand — it must leave no sensation of grit. Dissolve the aloe in rosewater, add the calcined copper sulfate and the camphor. Triturate all ingredients together with sufficient rosewater until the preparation coheres without separation and the colour is uniform. Apply with a fine stylus of miswāk wood or silver.
Dosage · Jur'ah
Topical application morning and evening; once daily in acute inflammation
Therapeutic indications · Manāfi'
Inflammation of the eye Opacity of the cornea Excessive lacrimation Strengthens the sight

Kāmūniyyat al-Hāḍim
كمونية الهاضم · Digestive compound of cumin · for disorders of the stomach and intestines
Ingredients · Adjzā'
IngredientQuantityPreparationSubst.
Cumin · kammūn 3 dramsRoasted, then powderedYes
Senna leaves · sanā MakkīMecca variety is most efficacious. Do not decoct — infuse in cold water for one hour to preserve the purgative principle. Discoloured leaves have lost their virtue. 2 dramsInfused in cold water, strainedNo
Ginger · zanjabīl 1 dramPowderedYes
Black salt · milḥ aswad ½ dramAs-isYes
Dried mint · na'nā' yābisFresh mint may replace dried in double quantity. Wild mint (fūdhanj) has stronger action — use it in half the quantity specified here. 2 dramsDried, powderedYes
Oxymel · sikanjabīn 2 ouncesAs vehicleYes
Preparation · Tadbīr
Roast the cumin in a dry iron vessel over moderate heat until fragrant; allow to cool before grinding. Infuse the senna leaves in cold water for one hour, then strain through linen and reduce the infusion over low heat by one half. Combine all powdered ingredients and blend with the oxymel and the senna reduction, working until the electuary is smooth and of spreadable consistency. The taste should be pleasantly bitter with a warming aftertaste.
Dosage · Jur'ah
2 drams taken after the evening meal with warm water; not upon an empty stomach
Therapeutic indications · Manāfi'
Sluggish digestion Tertian fever Liver obstruction Flatulence and colic
