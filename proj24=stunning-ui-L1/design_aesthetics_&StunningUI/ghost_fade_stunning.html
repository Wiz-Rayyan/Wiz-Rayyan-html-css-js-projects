You are scrolling
toward absence.

At the top of this page, everything is present. Solid. Legible. As you move through it, the interface begins to leave. Not dramatically — gradually, like attention drifting from a room you've already decided to exit.

opacity: 1 − t blur: t × 18px drift: t × −160px

"We make permanent things in a medium that was always meant to disappear."

The scroll gesture is interesting because it implies forward motion — progress, consumption, arrival somewhere. But what if arriving somewhere meant watching everything you came from dissolve behind you?

Interfaces usually resist this. They hold on. They remain. This one doesn't. It's practicing a kind of letting go — with you, whether you wanted to practice or not.

There's something true in it. Memory works like this. Attention works like this. The further you get from the beginning, the more you're relying on impressions rather than the thing itself.

You can scroll back. The page will return. It was never really gone — just less insistent about being seen.

By now you are barely here.
