Ma'jūn al-Qalb — Electuary of the Heart
معجون القلب · Compound cardiac restorative
Ingredients — Adjzā'
Ingredient Quantity Preparation Sub.
Rose petals, driedIf dried roses are unavailable, substitute fresh petals in double the quantity; avoid black-petalled varieties as they lack the warming virtue. 4 drams Powdered fine Yes
Saffron stigmasBeware adulteration with safflower (qurtum). Genuine saffron stains the finger yellow-orange and possesses a bitter taste. ½ dram Ground to paste No
Gold leaf (waraq al-dhahab) 2 grains Dissolved in rose-water No
Pearl powder (lū'lū') 1 dram Ground with amber Yes
Mastic (maṣṭakā)Store in a cool, dry location away from sunlight. Mastic softens in heat and must be powdered in winter months when firm. 2 drams Powdered Yes
Honey (raw, clarified) 3 ounces Lightly warmed Yes
Preparation — Tadbīr
Pound the rose petals and mastic to a fine powder. Dissolve the saffron in two spoons of rosewater over low heat until it becomes a homogeneous paste. Add the pearl powder and gold leaf. Combine all with the clarified honey, stirring continuously until the mixture is of electuary consistency. Store in a sealed glass vessel (qārūra).
Dosage — Jur'ah
1 dram upon waking, with warm rosewater or pomegranate juice
Therapeutic Indications — Manāfi'
Cardiac palpitation Febrile conditions Melancholy Strengthens vital spirit
Kuḥl al-'Ayn — Collyrium for the Eye
كحل العين · Ophthalmic preparation
Ingredients — Adjzā'
Ingredient Quantity Preparation Sub.
Antimony (ithmid)The finest ithmid is the Isfahani variety, dark in colour and without veins. Do not substitute with lead sulfide, which may harm the eye. 3 drams Ground to finest powder on porphyry stone No
Aloe succotrina (ṣabr) ½ dram Dissolved in rosewater Yes
Copper sulfate (zāj)Use in minimal quantity; excess causes irritation. The Cypriot variety (qubrusī) is considered superior to the Egyptian. 5 grains Calcined and powdered No
Camphor (kāfūr) 2 grains Powdered separately Yes
Rosewater 1 ounce To triturate Yes
Preparation — Tadbīr
Grind the antimony upon a smooth stone until no grit remains, testing upon the back of the hand. Dissolve the aloe in rosewater, add the calcined copper sulfate and camphor. Triturate all together until the mixture coheres without separation. Apply with a fine miswāk rod or silver stylus.
Dosage — Jur'ah
Applied topically morning and evening; once daily in acute inflammation
Therapeutic Indications — Manāfi'
Eye inflammation Opacity of the cornea Excessive tearing Strengthens sight
Kāmūniyyah — Compound of Cumin
الكمّونية · Digestive and febrifugal powder
Ingredients — Adjzā'
Ingredient Quantity Preparation Sub.
Cumin (kammūn) 3 drams Roasted, then powdered Yes
Senna leaves (sanā)Senna leaves from the Mecca variety (sanā Makkī) are the most efficacious. Avoid leaves discoloured by damp, as their purging power is diminished. 2 drams Infused in warm water No
Ginger (zanjabīl) 1 dram Powdered Yes
Black salt (milḥ aswad) ½ dram As-is Yes
Mint (na'na')Fresh mint may replace the dried variety in twice the quantity. Garden mint is preferred; wild mint (fūdhanj) has a stronger action and should be used in lesser amounts. 2 drams Dried, powdered Yes
Oxymel (sikanjabīn) 2 ounces Vehicle for electuary Yes
Preparation — Tadbīr
Roast the cumin in a dry iron vessel until fragrant, then allow to cool before grinding. Infuse the senna leaves in warm water for one hour, strain, and reduce the infusion by half. Combine all powders and blend with the oxymel and reduced senna decoction. The resulting electuary should be of soft, spreadable consistency.
Dosage — Jur'ah
2 drams taken after the evening meal with warm water
Therapeutic Indications — Manāfi'
Sluggish digestion Tertian fever Liver obstruction Flatulence Intestinal colic
