Median Cubital Vein
الوريد الكوعي المتوسط
Most-used site in Islamic medicine. Located at the antecubital fossa. Treats palpitation, bilious fever, and excess heat of the liver. Spiritually prescribed for pride and anger by cooling the sanguine humor.
200 dirhams · Spring preferred
Basilic Vein — Bāsīlīq
الوريد الباسيليق
The medial vein of the upper arm. Prescribed for ailments of the spleen, black bile, melancholy, and the spiritual disease of envy (ḥasad). Opening it dispels the cold stagnant humor darkening the heart.
Melancholy · Black bile · Envy
Cephalic Vein — Qīfāl
الوريد القيفال
Lateral forearm and upper arm. For ailments of the head, neck, eyes — headache, vertigo, dimness of sight. Spiritually treats heedlessness (ghafla) and confusion of faith clouding the rūḥ.
Head ailments · Spiritual clarity
Jugular Vein — Warīd
الوريد الوداجي
Great vein of the neck. Reserved for mania, epilepsy, extreme heat of the brain. Ibn Sina: caution required — only the experienced physician should attempt. Spiritually for states of violent heedlessness.
Mania · Brain fever · Caution
Saphenous Vein — Ṣāfin
الوريد الصافن
Long inner vein of the leg and ankle. For sciatica, gout, and chronic lower ailments. Drawing from the foot draws corrupt humor downward from the heart, easing grief and fear lodged in the lower nafs.
Lower body · Chronic grief · Fear
Temporal Artery — Ṣudghī
الشريان الصدغي
The vessel at the temple. For migraines and eye disease. Relieves pressure on the brain's anterior ventricle — seat of imagination (khayāl) — thus treating delusion and obsessive thought.
Migraine · Delusion · Imagination
