Istiʿārah · Metaphor
☽ Qurʾānic usage: 214× ʿIlm al-Bayān branch
A metaphor (istiʿārah) borrows the name of one thing and applies it to another, asserting identity rather than merely comparing — the vehicle of resemblance (wajh al-shabah) is suppressed.
زيدٌ أسدٌ في الشجاعة
"Zayd is a lion in courage" — the lion is attributed directly; no comparative particle.
Mushabbah Zayd
Mushabbah bihi Lion
Wajh al-shabah Courage
مفتاح الاستعارة — Metaphor Key 🔑
Zayd is a lion
→
Zayd embodies courage
The key turns the literal statement — Zayd is a lion — unlocking the suppressed vehicle of resemblance: Zayd possesses the courage of a lion. The particle of comparison (كـ, مثل) is absent; the two terms are asserted as identical.

المتنبي · al-Mutanabbī Abbasid · 915–965 CE
أَنَا الَّذي نَظَرَ الأَعْمَى إِلَى أَدَبِي
"I am he whose literary art even the blind man gazes upon"
Figure: istiʿārah taṣrīḥiyyah — "the blind man gazes" applies the verb of sight to one deprived of it, implying that the poet's eloquence transcends the faculty of vision itself. The vehicle (sight) is explicitly named; the tenor (intellectual appreciation) is suppressed.
المتنبي · al-Mutanabbī Abbasid · 915–965 CE
وَالخَيلُ وَاللَّيلُ وَالبَيداءُ تَعرِفُني
"The horses, the night, and the desert know me well"
Figure: istiʿārah makniyyah — cognition is attributed to inanimate entities (horses, night, desert). The animate quality is suppressed and signalled only by the predicate تعرفني. A celebrated instance of personification-as-metaphor in the Arab classical tradition.
أبو نواس · Abū Nuwās Abbasid · c. 756–814 CE
دَعْ عَنكَ لَومي فَإِنَّ اللَّومَ إِغراءُ
"Leave off your reproach — your reproach is but an enticement"
Figure: istiʿārah by predication — reproach (لوم), normally a deterrent, is re-denominated as enticement (إغراء). The speaker asserts identity between censure and encouragement, collapsing the opposition between rebuke and invitation.
أبو نواس · Abū Nuwās Abbasid · c. 756–814 CE
كَأَنَّ صُغرَى وَكُبرَى مِن فَوَاقِعِها — حَصبَاءُ دُرٍّ عَلَى أَرضٍ مِن الذَّهَبِ
"As if its small and large drops were pearls of hail scattered over a floor of gold"
Figure: tashbīh murakkab shaping into istiʿārah — raindrops compared to pearls, ground to gold. The extended vehicle (jewelled surface) fully supplants the literal scene, approaching istiʿārah tamthīliyyah (analogical metaphor over a compound image).
