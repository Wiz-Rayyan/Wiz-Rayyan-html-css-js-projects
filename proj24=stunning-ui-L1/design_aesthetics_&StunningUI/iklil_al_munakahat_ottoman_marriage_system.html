فتاوى النكاح Fatwa Ruling Library — Legal Opinions on Marriage
Minimum Mahr Requirement · حد المهر الأدنى
Hanafi Source: Fatawa-i-Alamgiri, Vol. 1
The minimum mahr in the Hanafi school is ten dirhams of standard silver weight. A contract specifying less than this amount is valid, but the wife is entitled to demand the minimum of ten dirhams. There is no stated maximum limit on the mahr amount.
Ibn Abidin: this applies to full-weight Ottoman dirham — not the debased silver of later periods.
Revocable Talaq and Iddah · الطلاق الرجعي والعدة
Hanafi Source: al-Hidaya, Marghinani
A first or second talaq is revocable (raj'i). The husband may revoke within the iddah period of three menstrual cycles without a new contract. If the iddah expires without revocation, the divorce becomes a minor irrevocable separation (ba'in sughra). A new nikah with new mahr is then required.
Practical note: revocation must be witnessed and documented in court registers.
Custody of Children After Divorce · حضانة الأطفال
Hanafi Shafi'i Source: Multaqa al-Abhur
In Hanafi law, the mother retains custody of sons until age seven and daughters until puberty. The father maintains the right to guardianship (wilaya) and financial maintenance throughout. If the mother remarries a non-mahram, custody transfers to the paternal grandmother or father.
Shafi'i school: after age seven or puberty, the child may choose parent.
Conditions for Plural Marriage · شروط تعدد الزوجات
Hanafi Source: Ottoman Qanun, 18th c.
The Quran permits up to four wives conditional on equal treatment and justice between them. Ottoman practice required documentation of financial capability and, in some periods, court notification of the existing wife. Equal provision of housing, maintenance, and conjugal time was legally enforceable.
18th-c. Istanbul courts: first wife could include stipulation (shart) in nikah nameh preventing second marriage.
Husband's Maintenance Obligation · نفقة الزوجة
Hanafi Source: Fatawa Qadikhan
Maintenance (nafaqa) includes food, clothing, housing, and domestic help proportional to the husband's means. Nafaqa begins at the marriage contract, not the consummation. If a husband fails to provide maintenance, the wife may petition the court for judicial divorce (faskh) after a specified period.
Arrears of nafaqa become a debt on the husband's estate, enforceable against heirs.
