Kalām · Theological Discourse
Al-Rāzī marshals twelve dalāʾil (proofs) for divine unity. The chief argument: multiplicity implies composition, and composition implies need for a composer — hence the Necessary Being is strictly one. He refutes the Muʿtazilite position that divine attributes are lā hiya wa-lā ghayruh (neither identical nor distinct) and defends the Ashʿarī view that attributes subsist in the essence Ashʿarī Kalām. The word Aḥad negates all multiplicity — numerical, conceptual, and ontological.
Falsafa · Philosophical Argument
Drawing on Ibn Sīnā's distinction between the Necessary and Contingent, al-Rāzī argues that 'Aḥad' excludes even the logical distinction between essence and existence that applies to contingent beings. The Necessary Being's māhiyya (quiddity) simply is its wujūd (existence) — no internal multiplicity is possible.

ʿUlūm Ṭabīʿiyya · Natural Sciences & Cosmology
Al-Rāzī correlates divine oneness with the unity of the cosmic order: the ʿArsh (Throne) and Kursī (Footstool) are the outermost spheres, and the harmony of the celestial spheres (aflāk) testifies to a single governing principle. The oneness of natural law — one gravity, one logic — points to one Lawgiver. Aristotelian cosmology
In the 12th century, Rāzī incorporates Galenic medicine, Ptolemaic astronomy, and Aristotelian physics. He notes that the human body's unity — organs serving one organism — mirrors divine unity. He cites the heart's primacy among organs as a cosmological analogy: as the heart is to the body, God is to creation. Medicine · Anatomy
al-Ṭabarī: focuses on transmitted reports (āthār) — "Aḥad" as proper name of God, not predicate. al-Zamakhsharī: Muʿtazilite reading — absolute unity refutes all anthropomorphism. Ibn Kathīr: Aḥad means God has no partner, rival, or equal — simple and direct. al-Rāzī: philosophical synthesis across all four methods simultaneously.
Shajara Jadaliyya · Argument Tree — "What does Aḥad exclude?"
What does "Aḥad" negate?
1. Numerical plurality
If God had a second, each would limit the other → neither is Necessary
∴ No second divine being
2. Internal composition
Objection: divine attributes appear multiple — power, knowledge, will…
Reply: attributes are not additional entities but modes of the one essence
∴ No real multiplicity in essence
3. Conceptual duality
Objection: even in thought we distinguish "God" from "His existence"
Reply: this is a limit of human intellect, not a real distinction in God
∴ Essence = Existence in God alone
"Aḥad" excludes every form of multiplicity — external, internal, and conceptual
Al-Rāzī, Mafātīḥ al-Ghayb, vol. 32 · Argument follows the burhān al-tamāniʿ (proof of mutual exclusion) and dalīl al-tarkīb (argument from composition)
ʿĀlam al-Aflāk · Cosmological Spheres — Rāzī's Celestial Order

Al-Rāzī's cosmology follows Ptolemaic-Aristotelian structure adapted for Qur'ānic theology. The verse's divine unity (tawḥīd) is reflected in the cosmic hierarchy: one ʿArsh (Throne) encompasses all, one Kursī (Footstool) supports it, and below are the seven samāwāt (heavens), each bearing its planet-angel.

The unity of this cosmological order — all moving in perfect concert — is Rāzī's burhān al-ʿināya (teleological proof): the sky's circular motion sustains the four elements below, which produce life. One design implies one Designer.
