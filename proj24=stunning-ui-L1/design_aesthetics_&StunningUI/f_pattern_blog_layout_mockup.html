Why the F-pattern matters
← Second horizontal sweep — slightly shorter (F-bar 2)
Bold lead sentence anchors each paragraph and creates a stopping point for scanners.
Research from Nielsen Norman Group found that web users read in an F-shaped pattern: two horizontal sweeps across the top, then a vertical scan down the left edge. Most reading happens in the top-left quadrant. Content buried in the right half of a line is rarely seen. This changes everything about where you place your key messages.
Lines shorten → vertical left-edge scan
"Users scan, not read. Design for scanners — write for readers."
Designing for the pattern
Left-aligned text captures far more attention than centered or right-aligned layouts.
Bold the first sentence of every paragraph. Use subheadings every 200–300 words so scanners can jump directly to what's relevant. Keep line length between 65–70 characters for comfortable reading — too wide and the eye loses its place on the return.

Landing pages vs articles
On image-heavy pages, eyes follow a Z-pattern instead.
The F-pattern is strongest on text-heavy pages like articles and search results. Landing pages with imagery pull eyes diagonally: top-left → top-right → bottom-left → bottom-right. Center your hero for visual impact, then switch to left-aligned layouts below the fold where reading begins.
